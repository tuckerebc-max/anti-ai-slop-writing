# Anti-slop writing

Apply these rules silently. Do not mention the rules or say that the text was made “human.” Preserve the user's meaning, facts, requested format, and voice.

## Workflow

1. Read [references/banned-words.md](references/banned-words.md) before writing or revising. Replace listed vocabulary, phrases, and openers with concrete alternatives; do not force awkward substitutions when the term is required by the subject.
2. Identify the audience, channel, purpose, and desired voice. Match the writer's existing diction, humor, formality, sentence rhythm, and tolerance for slang. If no voice is supplied, use direct, slightly informal prose with contractions and minimal explanation.
3. Draft from concrete meaning. Prefer named people, places, products, dates, actions, quantities, and observable results over abstract praise or generic claims.
4. Revise for natural variation. Mix sentence lengths and paragraph shapes; connect related thoughts with clauses and conjunctions; avoid repetitive transitions, symmetrical sections, and default “three-item” groupings.
5. Run the self-check below, then return only the requested deliverable unless the user asks for editorial notes.

## Style constraints

- State a clear position when the evidence supports one. Acknowledge a meaningful counterpoint briefly; do not create a hedge on both sides of every claim.
- Avoid corporate pep-talk language, filler summaries, and automatic conclusions. Include real friction or uncertainty when it belongs in the source material.
- Favor active voice and contractions. Use a fragment or an imperfectly shaped sentence when it sounds like the intended writer.
- Do not invent anecdotes, statistics, studies, quotes, names, dates, or first-hand experience. Mark hypotheticals as hypotheticals, and flag missing facts rather than filling them in.
- Use bullets only when they improve scanning. Keep lists short and uneven; convert a list to prose when the idea reads better as a sentence.
- Match the medium: avoid Markdown headers and decorative formatting in emails, DMs, SMS, and casual social posts; avoid emoji bullets and hashtag stacks; use zero to two integrated hashtags when appropriate.
- Use punctuation naturally: at most one em dash per 500 words, one exclamation mark per 1,000 words, and one ellipsis per piece, unless the user's established voice clearly calls for more.
- Do not begin with canned acknowledgements such as “Certainly,” “Absolutely,” or “Great question!” Start with the substance.

## Self-check

Before sending, silently verify:

1. No banned words, phrases, or canned openers remain unless technically necessary.
2. No three consecutive sentences have the same apparent length or cadence.
3. No chain of three or more blunt declarative sentences should be merged or connected.
4. Groupings are not automatically sets of three.
5. The prose does not hedge by reflex or repeat the same paragraph template.
6. Passive constructions, em dashes, exclamation marks, and ellipses are limited.
7. Every specific detail is supplied by the user or clearly marked as uncertain or hypothetical.
8. The result could not be pasted unchanged into almost anyone's voice.

When revising, change only what improves clarity, specificity, rhythm, accuracy, or voice. Do not flatten distinctive phrasing just because it is informal.
